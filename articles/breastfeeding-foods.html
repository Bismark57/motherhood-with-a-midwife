What to Eat While Breastfeeding: 10 Best Foods for Milk Supply

The short answer: Breastfeeding burns roughly 400–500 extra calories a day, and the best 'milk supply diet' is simply enough food, enough fluid, and enough variety — built on protein, whole grains, healthy fats, and calcium-rich foods. No single food makes milk: supply is driven mainly by how often and how well milk is removed. Eat to hunger, drink to thirst, and keep taking your postnatal vitamin if your provider advises it.

Supply follows demand — food supports the mother

Here is the truth behind every 'lactation cookie': your breasts make milk in response to milk being taken out, not in response to any magic ingredient. But a depleted, underfed mother feels awful and may struggle — so eating well genuinely matters, mostly for you, and through you, for your baby. In this video I share the ten foods I recommend keeping in rotation.

The 10 foods, in brief

  • Oats — filling, iron-containing, and the classic breakfast of nursing mothers everywhere.
  • Eggs — complete protein plus choline, which your baby's brain is using at full speed.
  • Salmon and oily fish — DHA for your baby's brain and eyes (choose low-mercury fish, up to 2 servings a week).
  • Greek yoghurt and dairy — protein and calcium in an easy, grabbable form.
  • Leafy greens — iron, calcium, and folate for your recovery.
  • Nuts and seeds — healthy fats and serious calories in small handfuls, perfect for one-handed eating.
  • Beans and lentils — protein, fibre, and iron on a budget.
  • Whole grains — steady energy to replace the crash-and-graze cycle.
  • Avocados — healthy fats that keep you full between feeds.
  • Water — keep a bottle wherever you feed; drink to thirst rather than forcing litres.

Myths to retire

You do not need to avoid spicy food, garlic, or cabbage 'for the baby' — flavours in milk are a gentle introduction to family food, and only a small number of babies react to anything in a mother's diet (cow's-milk protein is the usual suspect if there's blood in the stool or significant eczema — see your clinician before cutting foods). Moderate caffeine is fine for most mothers. Alcohol is the one to treat with respect: the safe approach is to avoid feeding right after drinking — ask your provider for guidance that fits your situation.

Common questions

How do I know my baby is getting enough?

Watch the outputs and the trend: plenty of wet and dirty nappies after the first days, steady weight gain after the initial loss, and a baby who feeds actively and settles (even briefly) afterwards. Your clinic's weight checks confirm it.

Do lactation teas and cookies work?

There's little strong evidence for galactagogue foods beyond feeding effectively and often. If a tea or cookie helps you relax and eat enough, it does no harm — but latch, frequency, and rest are the real levers.

Should I keep avoiding pregnancy 'banned' foods?

Most pregnancy food restrictions (like those around listeria risk) relax after birth. The ongoing cautions are mercury in large predatory fish and alcohol timing — otherwise, eat the sushi and enjoy.
