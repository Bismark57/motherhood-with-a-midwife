Early Pregnancy

Early Signs of Pregnancy: 5 Symptoms That Surprise Women Most

The short answer: A missed period is the famous sign — but many women notice changes before it: unusually sore breasts, deep exhaustion, a suddenly superhuman sense of smell, mild cramping or spotting, and emotional swings. These come from the hormone surge of early pregnancy, mainly progesterone and hCG, and they can begin as early as 1–2 weeks after conception.

1. Breasts that feel different — really different

Many women describe this as PMS breast tenderness turned up several notches: heavy, sore, tingling, sometimes uncomfortable even in a familiar bra. It can begin within a week or two of conception as blood flow and hormone levels climb.

2. Exhaustion that sleep doesn't fix

This is the one that surprises women most. Not ordinary tiredness — a bone-deep need to sleep, arriving in the middle of the day. Progesterone is a natural sedative, and your body is also busy building the placenta. If you're suddenly asleep on the sofa at 7 p.m., your body may be telling you something.

3. A nose like a bloodhound

Perfume you've worn for years turns your stomach. Coffee smells unbearable. Rising oestrogen heightens smell and taste — often paired with a metallic taste in the mouth or sudden food aversions. For many women this arrives before any nausea does.

4. Mild cramping or a little spotting

Around 6–12 days after ovulation, the fertilised egg implants in the womb lining. Some women feel light cramps or see a small amount of pink or brown spotting — implantation bleeding. It's lighter and shorter than a period. (Heavy bleeding or one-sided pain is different — get checked promptly; see the note below.)

5. Emotions running ahead of you

Tears at an advert. Irritation, then joy, then tears again. The hormonal shifts of early pregnancy genuinely affect mood — you're not imagining it, and you're not "being dramatic."

Get checked the same day if you have:

  • Heavy bleeding, or bleeding with clots
  • Sharp or one-sided pelvic pain
  • Shoulder-tip pain, dizziness, or fainting

These can be signs of an ectopic pregnancy or miscarriage and need urgent assessment — don't wait and wonder.

Pregnancy symptoms or PMS?

They overlap cruelly — sore breasts, cramps, moodiness, tiredness happen in both. Two clues: pregnancy symptoms tend to be stronger than your usual pre-period pattern, and they don't end with a period. Only a test settles it — from the first day of a missed period, home tests are reliable.

Common questions

How early can symptoms start?

Some women notice changes 1–2 weeks after conception — around or just before the missed period. Others feel nothing for weeks. Both are completely normal.

I have no symptoms. Should I worry?

No. A large minority of women sail through the first weeks feeling entirely themselves. Absence of symptoms is not a sign of a problem.

When should I take a pregnancy test?

From the first day of your missed period for the most reliable result. If it's negative but your period still doesn't come, test again in 2–3 days.
