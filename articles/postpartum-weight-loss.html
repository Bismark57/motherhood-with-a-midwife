After Birth

How to Lose Weight After Pregnancy: A Safe Postpartum Plan That Actually Works

The short answer: There is no race. Your body needs the first 6 weeks to heal; after your postnatal check, gentle, steady loss of about 0.5 kg (1 lb) a week is a safe pace for most women. What works is unglamorous: regular meals built on protein, fibre and whole foods, daily walking building up gradually, sleep whenever it's offered, and patience — many women find the last kilos only shift after weaning. Crash diets while breastfeeding are a no.

First, some honest numbers

You lose roughly 5–6 kg at the birth itself (baby, placenta, fluid). More fluid follows in the first two weeks. What remains came on over nine months, and the evidence — and every honest midwife — says to give it a similar season to come off. In this video I lay out the plan I give my own patients: heal first, then build habits that survive life with a newborn.

The plan, in brief

  • Weeks 0–6: heal. Rest, eat properly, walk gently as comfort allows. No dieting — your body is repairing and (if breastfeeding) producing milk.
  • After your postnatal check: build a daily walk, add strength work gradually, and structure meals: protein at every meal, vegetables filling half the plate, whole grains, and water at your side all day.
  • Guard your sleep. Sleep deprivation drives hunger hormones hard. Nap when the baby naps isn't a cliché — it's strategy.
  • Breastfeeding burns extra energy (roughly 400–500 kcal a day) — but it also makes many women ravenous. Feed the hunger with real meals, not grazing on sugar.
  • Check the hidden causes. If weight won't move despite honest effort, ask about thyroid checks and diastasis recti (tummy muscle separation) — both are common, treatable, and not your fault.

What to avoid

Skip the detox teas, waist trainers, and 1,200-calorie challenges — especially while breastfeeding, where severe restriction can dent your milk supply and your mood. And be careful whose 'bounce-back' you compare yourself to: genetics, birth type, sleep, and help at home differ wildly. Your only real competitor is last month's version of you.

Common questions

When can I exercise after a caesarean?

Walking from the first days, as comfort allows. Anything strenuous, core-loaded, or high-impact waits for your postnatal check and your clinician's clearance — usually around 6 weeks, sometimes longer.

Will breastfeeding make me lose weight?

For many women, yes — eventually. Some bodies hold onto a reserve until weaning, and that's a normal hormonal pattern, not a failure.

How much weight gain in pregnancy was 'normal'?

Guidelines vary by starting BMI — roughly 11–16 kg for women in the middle range. If you gained more, you're in very large company, and the plan above still applies exactly the same.
