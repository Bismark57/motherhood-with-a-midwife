Early Pregnancy

Morning Sickness: 7 Remedies That Actually Work

The short answer: Morning sickness is driven by rising pregnancy hormones and usually settles by weeks 12–14. What helps most: small, frequent meals, ginger, keeping something dry by the bed for before you get up, fresh air and avoiding trigger smells, cold or room-temperature foods, staying hydrated in small sips, and rest — tiredness makes nausea worse. If you can't keep fluids down for 24 hours, you're losing weight, or you feel dizzy or faint, contact your care provider the same day.

Why it happens

Despite the name, morning sickness can arrive morning, noon, or night. It tracks the rise of hCG and oestrogen in the first trimester — which is why it usually peaks around week 9 and fades as the placenta takes over hormone production. It's miserable, but in most pregnancies it's also a sign that hormone levels are rising as they should. In this video I walk through the seven remedies I recommend first, in the order I would try them.

The 7 remedies, in brief

  • Eat little and often. An empty stomach is a nauseous stomach. Keep plain snacks — crackers, toast, dry cereal — within reach all day.
  • The bedside snack. Eat something dry before you even sit up in the morning, then wait a few minutes before getting up.
  • Ginger. Ginger tea, ginger biscuits, or ginger ale with real ginger — one of the best-studied natural remedies for pregnancy nausea.
  • Manage smells. Cooking smells are the classic trigger. Cold foods smell less; let someone else cook when you can, and open windows.
  • Sip, don't gulp. Small sips of water, ice chips, or ice lollies through the day. Big drinks on an empty stomach often come straight back.
  • Rest. Nausea is measurably worse when you're exhausted. Lie down when you can — this is not the season for pushing through.
  • Acupressure bands. Wrist bands for the P6 point help some women; they're cheap, safe, and worth trying.

When it's more than morning sickness

A small number of women develop hyperemesis gravidarum — severe vomiting that causes dehydration and weight loss. Contact your provider the same day if you cannot keep any fluid down for 24 hours, you're losing weight, your urine is very dark or scant, or you feel dizzy or faint. There are safe prescription options — you do not have to just endure it.

Common questions

When does morning sickness start and end?

It typically begins around week 6, peaks around week 9, and eases by weeks 12–14 for most women. A smaller number feel it into the second half of pregnancy — if that's you, raise it at your appointments rather than suffering quietly.

Can morning sickness harm my baby?

Ordinary morning sickness does not harm your baby — babies take what they need. What matters is severe vomiting with dehydration and weight loss, which is why the warning signs above deserve a same-day call.

Are anti-nausea medicines safe in pregnancy?

Some are routinely prescribed in pregnancy when remedies aren't enough. Only take what's prescribed or approved by your own provider — don't self-medicate from the pharmacy shelf.
