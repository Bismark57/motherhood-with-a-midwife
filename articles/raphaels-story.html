Our Story

Raphael's Story: Born at 800 Grams

The short version: My pregnancy became dangerous — preeclampsia and bleeding — and my son Raphael had to be born two months early, weighing just 800 grams. What followed was a NICU journey of fear, faith, and tiny victories. This is our story, and it's the reason this website and my channel exist.

A pregnancy that changed course

I practised as a midwife in Ghana. I had cared for many mothers through complicated pregnancies — and then it was my turn. My pregnancy developed preeclampsia, the blood-pressure condition I now teach every mother to watch for, and it came with bleeding. The warning signs I had explained to so many women were suddenly my own story.

It's why I speak about preeclampsia the way I do on this site: its early symptoms are easy to dismiss — I know exactly how easy. Please read the preeclampsia warning signs guide and take them seriously, even when they seem small.

Born two months early

For Raphael's safety and mine, he had to come early — two months before his time. He arrived weighing 800 grams: less than a bag of sugar. Nothing in my training prepared me for seeing my own baby that small. In that moment I wasn't a midwife. I was a mother, like every mother I've ever cared for.

The NICU days

The neonatal intensive care unit became our world: the incubator, the monitors, the glass between us and our son. The days were measured in grams gained, in feeds tolerated, in breaths taken on his own. If you are living those days right now, hear this from a mother who has been exactly where you are: you are not alone, and small progress is still progress.

My husband and I held on — to each other, to our faith, and to every tiny sign that our boy was fighting. He was. He is.

Why we share this

Raphael came home. He is growing, thriving, and filling our house with life. But we never forgot the mothers still sitting beside incubators, or the ones whose pregnancies are quietly turning dangerous without them knowing.

That's why I make the From Womb to World videos, and why this site exists: so that what we learned the hard way can protect your family the easier way. Start with the guides, watch along on YouTube, and please — never ignore a warning sign.

If you're a NICU parent today

Rest when you can. Ask the nurses every question you have — there are no foolish ones. And let people help you. Your baby needs you standing, and standing takes a village. We are cheering for your family.
