Pregnancy

10 Shocking Pregnancy Truths Nobody Warns You About!

The short answer: Pregnancy changes far more than your bump. In this honest countdown, Sandra shares 10 truths nobody warns you about — from feet that can permanently change size, vivid and weird dreams and a superhuman sense of smell, to lightning crotch, postpartum hair shedding and the first bowel movement after birth. Most of them are completely normal. One is not: severe itching on your palms and soles can be a sign of cholestasis and should always be reported to your care provider. Watch to learn what is normal, what to expect — and the one symptom to never ignore.

Upload date: November 6, 2026

10. Your feet can permanently change size

The countdown starts with a surprise many mothers only notice when their old shoes no longer fit after the baby arrives. During pregnancy, hormones loosen ligaments throughout your body — including in your feet — while the extra weight of pregnancy can flatten your arches a little. For some women the change is temporary swelling; for others, their feet stay a half size or more larger for good. If your shoes feel tight late in pregnancy, believe your feet, not your old shoe size.

9. Vivid and weird pregnancy dreams

Many pregnant women report dreams that are unusually vivid, detailed and strange — dreams about the baby, about birth, and about completely unexpected things. Hormonal changes, lighter and more broken sleep, and waking more often in the night (which makes dreams easier to remember) all play a part. Strange dreams can feel unsettling, but on their own they are a very common and normal part of pregnancy.

8. A superhuman sense of smell — and morning sickness

Early in pregnancy, smells that never bothered you before — coffee, cooking, perfume, the fridge — can suddenly seem overwhelmingly strong. This heightened sense of smell is closely tied to the nausea and morning sickness of the first trimester, and for many women it is one of the very first signs that something has changed. It usually eases as pregnancy goes on. Sandra's dedicated guide to morning sickness, earlier in this learning path, covers the remedies that genuinely help.

7. Severe itching (palms and soles): the cholestasis warning

This is the one truth in the countdown that can actually matter medically — and the reason this video belongs in every mother's watching. Mild itching from stretching skin is common in pregnancy. But severe itching, especially on the palms of your hands and the soles of your feet, can be a symptom of cholestasis of pregnancy, a liver condition that needs prompt assessment. Do not dismiss intense palm and sole itching as "just pregnancy." Call your doctor, midwife or care provider and describe it — this is exactly the kind of symptom they want to hear about early.

6. Lightning crotch and pelvic nerve pain

A sudden, sharp, shooting pain low in the pelvis — brief, startling, and gone as quickly as it came — is known to many mothers as "lightning crotch." As your baby grows and settles lower, pressure on pelvic nerves can produce these short jolts, especially in later pregnancy. It is usually normal, if unpleasant. Pain that is constant, worsening, or accompanied by bleeding, leaking fluid or regular contractions is different — that should always be checked promptly.

5. Pregnancy hair versus postpartum hair shedding

Pregnancy hormones often slow normal hair shedding, so many women enjoy thicker-feeling hair while they are pregnant. What nobody warns you about is the reversal: a few months after birth, the hormones shift and all that held-back hair sheds at once. Postpartum hair shedding can look alarming in the shower and on your brush, but it is a normal, temporary phase for most mothers — your hair is returning to its usual cycle, not disappearing for good.

4. The first postpartum bowel movement

Almost no one talks about it beforehand, and almost every new mother thinks about it afterwards: the first bowel movement after birth can feel daunting, whether you had a vaginal birth with stitches or a caesarean. The video names it honestly so it is not a frightening surprise. Gentle habits help — do not delay when you feel the urge, keep up fluids and fibre as you are able, and tell your care team if constipation or pain is a problem rather than suffering in silence.

3. Big pregnancy emotions and hormonal mood swings

Pregnancy hormones, broken sleep, physical discomfort and the sheer size of the life change ahead can combine into powerful mood swings — tears, irritability and joy sometimes all in the same hour. Big emotions in pregnancy are common and they are not a character flaw. Rest, honesty with the people close to you, and support all help. If low mood or anxiety is persistent, overwhelming, or stopping you coping day to day, tell your care provider — support for perinatal mental health is part of good care, not an extra.

2. Belly touching and unsolicited advice

Once your bump shows, something strange happens in public: hands reach for your belly and advice arrives unasked — from family, friends and complete strangers. The video acknowledges what many mothers feel but rarely say out loud: it is your body, and it is allowed to bother you. A polite hand on your own bump, a step back, or a simple "please don't" is enough. You do not owe anyone access to your belly, and you do not have to act on advice that does not sit right — your care team is the place for real questions.

1. Why you might actually miss being pregnant

The countdown ends with the truth that surprises mothers most: after all the symptoms, the discomfort and the waiting, you may find that you miss being pregnant — the kicks and movements that were yours alone, the closeness of carrying your baby with you everywhere, and that particular season of expectation. Missing pregnancy does not mean the hard parts were not hard. It means the journey meant something — and it is a completely normal feeling to carry into new motherhood.

The one symptom to never ignore

Nine of these ten truths are normal parts of pregnancy and early motherhood. Keep the exception front of mind:

  • Severe itching on your palms and soles — possible cholestasis. Call your care provider and describe it; do not wait for your next routine appointment.
  • And as in every guide on this site: bleeding, reduced baby movements, severe headache, vision changes, sudden swelling, leaking fluid, or anything that frightens you always means contacting your care team or going in right away.

Common questions

Can your feet really grow permanently during pregnancy?

Yes, for some women. Pregnancy hormones loosen ligaments and the extra weight can flatten the arches, so feet may end up longer or wider than before — sometimes by half a shoe size or more, and sometimes permanently. Swelling alone goes down after birth; a lasting change in shoe size usually reflects the ligament and arch changes the video describes.

When is itching in pregnancy dangerous?

Mild itching over stretching skin is common. Severe itching — particularly on the palms of your hands and the soles of your feet — is different: it can be a symptom of cholestasis of pregnancy and should always be reported to your doctor, midwife or care provider promptly for assessment, rather than waited out.

What is lightning crotch?

A brief, sharp, shooting pain low in the pelvis, usually caused by the growing baby pressing on pelvic nerves, most often in later pregnancy. Individual jolts that pass quickly are usually normal. Constant, worsening pain, or pain with bleeding, leaking fluid or regular contractions, should be checked promptly.

Is postpartum hair shedding normal?

Yes. Pregnancy hormones slow normal shedding, so hair often feels thicker during pregnancy; a few months after birth the accumulated hair sheds, which can look dramatic but is normally temporary as your hair returns to its usual cycle.

Is it normal to miss being pregnant after the baby is born?

Very normal — and it is the closing truth of this video. Many mothers miss the movements, the closeness and the anticipation of pregnancy even while they are glad the uncomfortable parts are over and delighted to be holding their baby. Both feelings can be true at once.
