Your Baby

From Conception to Birth: The Incredible 9-Month Journey

The short answer: A baby begins when a sperm fertilises an egg in the fallopian tube. The fertilised egg divides as it travels, implants in the womb about a week later, and builds the placenta — the organ that will feed and oxygenate your baby for nine months. Then come three trimesters of astonishing construction, ending in birth.

Conception: where it all starts

At ovulation, an egg is released and — if sperm are present — fertilisation usually happens in the fallopian tube. The single fertilised cell carries the complete genetic plan: half from mother, half from father. It begins dividing immediately — 1 cell becomes 2, then 4, then a ball of cells — while travelling toward the womb over about 5–6 days.

Implantation and the placenta

Around day 6–10 after fertilisation, the cluster of cells burrows into the womb lining — implantation. Some women notice a little spotting at this point. Part of the cluster becomes the baby; part becomes the placenta, the lifeline that will pass oxygen and food from your blood to your baby and carry waste away. The pregnancy hormone hCG — the one a pregnancy test detects — is produced from here.

The three trimesters

  • First (to week 12): The blueprint phase. Heart, brain, spinal cord, organs, arms and legs all form. By week 12, your baby has a recognisably human shape. See the week-by-week guide.
  • Second (weeks 13–27): The growth phase. Movements begin, senses wake up, and baby starts to look like the newborn you'll meet.
  • Third (weeks 28–40): The finishing phase. Fat is laid down, lungs mature, the brain wires itself at breathtaking speed, and baby settles head-down, ready.

Birth: the handover

When the time comes, hormones signal your womb to contract, the cervix opens, and your baby makes the journey into the world — through the birth canal, or by caesarean. With the first breath, the baby's circulation rearranges itself in seconds: the lungs take over the job the placenta did for nine months. It is the most dramatic handover in all of biology — and it happens in your arms.

The part that still amazes me

I've attended many births, and I still marvel at this: in nine months, from two cells joining, your body builds a complete human being — roughly 300 million cells a day at the fastest point — mostly while you sleep, eat, and go about your life. You are doing something extraordinary right now, even on the days it just feels like heartburn.

Common questions

How soon after conception can I test positive?

Most home tests work from the first day of a missed period — about two weeks after conception — when hCG has risen enough. Testing earlier gives many false negatives.

Can I feel implantation?

Usually not. Some women notice light spotting or mild cramping around 6–12 days after ovulation, but most feel nothing at all — both are normal.

When is my due date calculated from?

From the first day of your last period, add 280 days (40 weeks). An early ultrasound can refine the date, especially if your cycle is irregular.
